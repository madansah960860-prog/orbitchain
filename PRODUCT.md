# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

US consumers who already own Apple hardware and need the parts around it — a
replacement braided cable, a band that survives a gym, a case that keeps MagSafe
working. They arrive from search or an ad with a specific object in mind, often
on a phone, often mid-task. They are not browsing for inspiration; they are
resolving a small, concrete annoyance.

Secondary: gift buyers who know the recipient's device but not the accessory
ecosystem, and need compatibility made obvious.

The job: *"Confirm this exact part fits my exact device, confirm it is genuine,
and get it moving today."*

## Product Purpose

Orbit Chain Co. is an independent, authorized third-party reseller of Apple
accessories. It is not Apple and does not pretend to be. It exists because the
accessory aisle is where counterfeits concentrate — the store's entire reason to
exist is that a buyer can trust what arrives in the box.

Success looks like: compatibility answered before it's asked, provenance stated
plainly, and a checkout that never surprises anyone on price.

## Brand Personality

**Machined. Plainspoken. Unhurried.**

Voice is a good parts counter: it knows the catalog cold, tells you when the
cheaper option is the right one, and never oversells. Specs are given as facts,
not as marketing. No superlatives that can't be measured — which is also what
Google Ads policy requires, so honesty and compliance point the same direction.

Emotional goal: *confidence*. The visitor should feel the way they feel holding
a well-made aluminum object — that someone cared about the tolerances.

## Anti-references

- **Apple.com pastiche.** Any design that could be mistaken for Apple's own
  store is both a trademark problem and a trust problem. We look adjacent to the
  hardware, never like its manufacturer.
- **Gadget-blog neon.** RGB gradients, "MEGA SALE" starbursts, fake countdown
  timers, strikethrough prices that were never real. Ads-policy poison.
- **Dropship grey.** Unbranded white grid, stock model photos, no address, no
  returns policy, no phone number.
- **Editorial-magazine affectation.** Display-serif drop caps and ruled columns.
  This is a hardware counter, not a quarterly.

## Design Principles

1. **Compatibility is the primary content.** On every product surface, "does
   this fit my device" outranks price, photography, and copy. If the visitor has
   to scroll to find it, the page failed.
2. **State the relationship, everywhere it matters.** Independent reseller, not
   Apple. In the footer, on the about page, and anywhere a trademark appears.
   Repetition here is a feature, not clutter.
3. **Glass is a material, not a filter.** Surfaces catch light on their top edge
   the way anodized aluminum does. If a panel has nothing worth blurring behind
   it, it isn't glass — it's a grey box, and it should be solid instead.
4. **No number moves without a reason.** Prices, stock counts, and delivery
   dates are facts. No invented urgency, no fake scarcity, no decorative
   animation on commercial figures.
5. **Motion follows the object.** Things that are magnetic snap. Things that are
   woven settle. Transitions carry the product image between pages rather than
   redrawing it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified against the actual
  token values rather than assumed.
- Glass panels must keep their contrast floor when the backdrop behind them is
  at its brightest — contrast is checked against the worst case, not the average.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` replaces every transform/scroll animation with an
  instant or crossfade state. Nothing is gated behind an animation that may
  never run.
- Full keyboard path through browse → product → cart → checkout, with visible
  focus rings that survive the glass treatment.
- Compatibility and stock state are never communicated by color alone; each
  carries text and a distinct icon shape.
